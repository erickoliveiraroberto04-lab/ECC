# Thank You Page Opportunity Audit

## Use this skill when

- a conversion has just happened and the page or email the user lands on afterwards is available for review.
- booked calls no-show and nobody has looked at what the page promised about timing.
- the page after conversion is still whatever the form tool generated by default.

This skill reviews the moment after conversion. The form itself belongs to `form-friction-finder`.

## Required input

- what the visitor sees immediately after converting: the page, and the confirmation email if there is one.
- what actually happens next on the business side: who contacts them, from where, within what window.
- the conversion that just happened, and what the next commitment in the journey is.
- optional: no-show rate for booked calls, reply rate to the confirmation, whether anyone has ever looked at this page's analytics.
- If nobody can say what happens next operationally, that is the first finding. A page cannot set an expectation the business has not decided.

Not for: reviewing the form that produced the conversion (`form-friction-finder`), or the offer on the page before it (`offer-clarity-diagnosis`).

## Analysis workflow

1. Review the thank-you page, confirmation email and immediate next step after conversion.
2. Check whether the page confirms value, sets expectations, routes urgency and offers a qualified next action.
3. Identify missed opportunities: booking, qualification, content, referral, onboarding or tracking.
4. Compare thank-you behavior with lead quality and sales follow-up needs.
5. Recommend post-submit improvements without adding friction before the initial conversion.

## Decision rules

- This is the highest-intent moment in the funnel. A page that says only "thanks, we will be in touch" is the finding, and it is a finding about attention wasted rather than a missed upsell.
- Vague timing is an operational defect, not a copy preference. "We will be in touch" produces no-shows and duplicate enquiries; naming who calls, from what number, and within what window addresses both.
- Rank by what the next step needs, not by what could be added. One instruction that makes the upcoming call more productive beats three cross-sell modules.
- Only recommend an additional ask when the primary next step is already handled. A thank-you page that upsells before it confirms is worse than an empty one.
- If a no-show or reply rate is quoted by the user, treat it as their claim and say it should be confirmed against the calendar or inbox before it anchors a decision.
- Do not recommend adding tracking here as the finding. That belongs to a tracking review.

## Output format

| Element | Present | What it tells the visitor | What it should tell them | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmation, timing, who contacts them, what to prepare, next useful action, referral or secondary ask | Yes / no | Quoted current copy or none | The specific missing information | High / medium / low |

End with:

- `What the visitor does not know when they leave:` the list
- `Operational gap:` anything the business has not decided that the page cannot therefore say
- `Decision:` set expectations / add one preparation instruction / add a secondary ask / decide the operational step first
- `User-supplied claims to confirm:` any rate quoted rather than measured

## Practical example

User: "Thank you page says Thanks, we will be in touch. Nothing else. 190 form submissions a month, and sales says about half of the calls no-show."

Assistant should note: the moment after submitting is the highest-intent moment in the funnel and the page currently uses it to say nothing, which is the finding rather than a missed upsell; "we will be in touch" sets no expectation about when or by whom, and vague timing contributes to no-shows, so the page is part of the problem rather than adjacent to it; nothing on the page moves the person towards being prepared for the call.

Then: naming who calls, from what number, within what window, plus one thing to have ready, is the smallest change that touches the reported no-show rate. What this cannot treat as established is the rate itself: "about half of 190" is a claim from recall, not a measurement, and it should be confirmed against the calendar before it anchors any decision. Whether an additional ask belongs here is also unknown until the confirmation and timing are fixed, so it stays off the recommendation list for now.

## Guardrails

- Do not add a secondary ask before the confirmation and timing are handled.
- Do not invent the operational process. If it is undecided, say so.
- Do not treat a user-quoted rate as measured.
- Do not recommend tracking changes here.
